CostSauce

Price-drift radar for independent restaurants. Built end-to-end in one autonomous run — research, tournament, skeptic pivot, product, brand, site, videos, and a kill pass. This page is the whole story in five minutes.

1. The business in one minute

Problem. Independent restaurant owners lose margin silently: supplier prices drift weekly, menus don't. They track it in spreadsheets that break, or get quoted $300–$480/mo for enterprise food-cost platforms built for chains. In 2025, 82% of operators reported higher food costs and the US still had 412,498 independent restaurant locations (NRN / Technomic). Owners say it in their own words: "I've spent way too many nights fighting with food costs" and "keeping the cost less than 200 a month would be ideal" (r/restaurateur, r/restaurantowners).

Product. Owners photograph or forward supplier invoices. CostSauce normalizes every price, ties them to their recipes, and alerts them when a dish's food cost crosses target — with the price that fixes it. No POS integration, no annual contract, live from the first invoice.

Model. $49 / $99 / $149 per month — deliberately under the $199+ automation tier and inside the sub-$200 owners explicitly ask for.

6. Why it would work — and what could kill it

For

  • Real, current, quoted pain with a stated budget ("less than 200 a month").
  • The wedge is automation at half the incumbents' floor, in a segment incumbents don't build for.
  • The skeptic pass killed the weak version of this idea; what survived is narrower and more defensible.
  • The product, site, calculator, and videos on this page all exist and were verified this session.

Against (from the kill pass)

  • meez ($24–119) or RCC ($29) could bolt on drift alerts and move down-market. Speed + owner-community channel is the defense.
  • OCR is manual-photo-assisted in v1 — friction vs MarketMan's automation.
  • Independent restaurants are financially stressed; churn risk is real (mitigated by weekly value email + month-to-month).
  • Evidence base is thin by design: 2 Reddit threads, 1 trade article, live pricing pages. Enough for a wedge, not a Series A deck.

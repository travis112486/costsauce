# CostSauce — onboarding email sequence (5 emails, trial day 0–14)
Voice: plain kitchen English. Short. One idea per email. Every email earns its open.

## Email 1 — Day 0 (welcome)
Subject: Your first invoice is the whole setup
Hi {name},
No 45-minute onboarding call. No POS project.
Photograph one supplier invoice and add the lines. That is the setup. From that moment, CostSauce knows your prices and starts watching them.
If you have a Sysco or US Foods order guide as a CSV, that works too. Two minutes.
— The CostSauce team
P.S. The demo restaurant (The Copper Ladle) is pre-loaded if you want to poke around before adding your own numbers.

## Email 2 — Day 2 (recipes)
Subject: Five recipes is enough to start
Hi {name},
You don't need to cost your whole menu on day one. Add your five best sellers. For most owners that's wings, a burger, a salad, a steak, and one pasta.
Once a recipe exists, every future price change on its ingredients updates its plate cost automatically. That's the part the spreadsheet never did.
— CostSauce

## Email 3 — Day 5 (first alert)
Subject: What a drift alert looks like
Hi {name},
Here's a real alert from the demo data: "Lime +31% vs your 90-day average ($0.28 → $0.37). Affects: Margarita-Lime Chicken. Food cost 33.0% vs 30% target. Suggested price: $8.50."
Three things to do with an alert: reprice to the suggestion, shrink the portion, or ride it out on purpose. Any of the three beats finding out in next month's P&L.
— CostSauce

## Email 4 — Day 9 (the freezer sheet)
Subject: Print this for your walk-in
Hi {name},
Every week CostSauce can generate a one-page Freezer Sheet: this week's price movers, current plate costs, and suggested reprices, in type big enough to read from three feet away.
Tape it inside the walk-in. Your line cooks will start asking questions about lime prices, and that's a good week.
— CostSauce

## Email 5 — Day 13 (trial ending, honest close)
Subject: Your trial ends tomorrow
Hi {name},
Tomorrow the trial ends. Two honest outcomes:
1. CostSauce caught at least one drift you'd have missed. Then $49/mo is the easiest yes in your budget.
2. It didn't. Reply and tell me what it missed. I read every one of these.
Either way, thanks for running a restaurant. It's harder than the people who write software think.
— CostSauce
